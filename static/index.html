Make a backing track

  1. Paste a YouTube or Spotify link, or drop in a song or video.
  2. It's split into vocals, sax/brass, guitar, piano, bass, drums and the rest.
  3. Mute your part, slow it down or change key, loop the hard bit.
  4. Hit ● to record yourself over it, then export.

One-time setup: install the helper

Songs are split by AI models that run on your own Mac — nothing is uploaded. A small helper app does that work in the background. Install it once and this website will use it from then on.

  1. Open Terminal (in Applications → Utilities).
  2. Paste this and press Return:
  3. Wait while it installs (about 5–10 minutes; it downloads ~1 GB). This page connects by itself when it's done.

Needs a Mac (Apple Silicon recommended — Intel works but is slow), about 3 GB of free space and Homebrew (the installer tells you if it's missing).

Already installed? Open Backing Track Studio Helper from your Applications folder. If your browser asks whether this site may access devices on your local network, choose Allow — that's how it talks to the helper. If you use Safari and it won't connect, try Chrome.

AA/B: compare with the original song

M · , .Add a marker · jump to previous / next marker
Z · Shift+ZZoom in (to the loop, if there is one) · show the whole song
MouseClick a waveform or the bar ruler to jump · drag to select a section (snaps to beats; hold Alt for free selection) · drag a section's edge to adjust it · double-click a slider to reset it · double-click a take's name to rename it · pinch or ⌘-scroll to zoom, scroll sideways to move · double-click the ruler to add a marker; Shift-click a marker to loop that section · double-click the song title to rename it
